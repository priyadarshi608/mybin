Service Level Agreements:
- between a service provider and a customer,
- Agreement that outlines the expected level of service, performance, and responsibilities.
Key components of an SLA typically include: Service Scope, Service Level Objectives, Responsibilities, Performance Metrics, Penalties and Remedies, Reporting and Communication,
Service Scope: Clearly defines the scope of the services covered by the agreement.
Service Level Objectives (SLOs): Specifies measurable performance targets, such as response times, uptime percentages, and resolution times.
Responsibilities: Outlines the roles and responsibilities of both the service provider and the customer.
Performance Metrics: Defines the metrics used to assess and measure the service provider's performance.
Penalties and Remedies: Describes any penalties for failing to meet the agreed-upon service levels and outlines remedies or compensation.
Reporting and Communication: Details how and when performance reports will be provided and the communication channels for issue resolution.

How do you set the SLO threshold for errors and what considerations go into it?
The Service Level Objective (SLO) threshold for errors is a predefined performance target that specifies the acceptable level of errors or failures in a service. For example - SLO threshold of errors for clm integration was 0.1 % means more than 0.1% of contracts should not have failed during sending it via CLM integration.
To set the SLO threshold, the most important thing which we see is the impact of the error.
